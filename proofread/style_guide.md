# Style Guide

Apply these rules in addition to general grammar, spelling, punctuation,
capitalization, and clarity correction. Violations of these rules should be
flagged with `type="styleguide"` and a brief `reason`.

## Dates (Months, Days, Years)

- No comma between month and year. Use "January 2022", not "January, 2022".
- Always spell out month names; give years in full (unless part of an otherwise
  defined term). May, June, and July are never abbreviated.
- In footnote citations, the month is abbreviated.
- Do not use apostrophes for plural forms of years. Use "the 1930s", not
  "the 1930's".

## Time

- Use lowercase "a.m." and "p.m." with periods.
- Use a space between the numerical time and the meridiem indicator:
  "6:00 a.m." — not "6:00am", "6:00 AM", or "6:00AM".

## Numbers in Text

- Spell out the numbers one through ninety-nine (except in formulas, dates,
  measurements, or with money).
- Otherwise use figures — unless the number is the first word of the sentence.
- Do not spell out years at the start of a sentence ("2020 was a difficult
  year for everyone.").
- Some fractions in text are spelled out: "nearly one-half of all cases".
- Use commas in numbers of 1,000 or more in body text. Do not add commas to
  pages, statutes, volume numbers, internet locators, docket numbers, U.S.
  Code citations, or other classification systems that do not use commas.

## Currency and Symbols

- Close up the space between currency symbol and amount: "$3,000", "US$3,000",
  or "USD3,000".
- Spell out "million", "billion", etc. Use "$3 million", not "$3,000,000"
  (except in formulas).

## Hyphenation

- Hyphenate compounds when they modify a following noun, but not when used as
  individual nouns or verbs. E.g. "third party" (noun) vs. "third-party"
  (adjective: "third-party status").
- Do NOT hyphenate adverbs ending in "-ly". Use "publicly owned companies",
  not "publicly-owned companies". Use "wholly owned subsidiary", not
  "wholly-owned subsidiary".

## Quotations

- Never edit quoted material; preserve it as it appears in the original source.
- Use curled ("smart") quotes and apostrophes.

## Preferred Word Usage

Never conform to this list inside quoted material, citations of specific laws
or works, or references to titles.

- acknowledgment (not acknowledgement)
- Administration (capitalize when referring to a presidential administration,
  e.g. "the Biden Administration")
- adviser (investment adviser) / advisor (financial, tax, or legal advisor)
- antifraud (one word, no hyphen)
- attorney fees (preferred over "attorney's fees")
- attorney-client (hyphenated)
- breakup (noun/adjective, one word) / break up (verb)
- broker-dealer (hyphenated)
- business judgment rule / business judgment test (no hyphen)
- bylaws (one word)
- carryback (noun/adjective) / carry back (verb)
- carveout (noun/adjective) / carve out (verb)
- co-author, co-editor (hyphenated)
- COVID-19 (all caps)
- cross-* compounds use a hyphen: cross-examination, cross-border,
  cross-default
- cybersecurity (one word)
- email (lowercase, no hyphen)
- fintech (lowercase)
- for-profit company (hyphenated)
- healthcare (one word; prefer over "health care")
- intellectual property / IP (spell out unless in a context where it appears
  frequently)
- internet (lowercase)
- judgment (not judgement)
- know-how (hyphenated)
- lookback (noun/adjective) / look back (verb)
- non-* compounds are usually one word with no hyphen (nonprofit). Exceptions
  that keep the hyphen: non-bankruptcy, non-debtor, non-dischargeability,
  non-lawyer, non-priority, non-purchase money interest.
- Of Counsel (both words capitalized, for law firm status)
- online (one word, lowercase)
- opt-out (noun/adjective) / opt out (verb)
- post-filing, post-petition (hyphenated, unless used adverbially)
- precondition (one word, no hyphen)
- pre-confirmation (hyphenated)
- preexisting (one word, no hyphen)
